What we do

Most of what gets called an ML club is a semester of slides. We run the other way around: you join a team in week three and you are writing training code by week four. Reading happens because a project needs it, not because it was on a syllabus.

Teams are deliberately small and deliberately mixed — a sophomore who has only ever touched scikit-learn sits next to someone who has been fine-tuning since high school. We take students from the Fu Foundation School, from Columbia College, from Barnard, and from GS. No prior research is expected. A willingness to be confused in public is.

How a semester runs

Fourteen weeks
WEEKS 1–2

Coffee chats

Twenty minutes at Joe on 120th. No resume screen, no whiteboard interview. We are trying to find out what you have actually built and what you want to build next.

WEEK 3

Pitch night

Anyone can pitch, members and newcomers alike. Teams form around the ideas that survive a room full of questions. Roughly half the pitches do not.

WEEKS 4–13

Build

Weekly standups in Mudd, a Thursday paper-reading group, and a shared compute pool. Leads keep teams honest about scope in week seven, when scope always slips.

WEEK 14

Demo night

Live demos in front of everyone, including the parts that did not work. A project that failed interestingly gets more questions than one that played it safe.
